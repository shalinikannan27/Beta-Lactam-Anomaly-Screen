Methodology & Research Framework

An overview of the computational strategies, machine learning architectures, and genomic datasets powering the AMR Atlas prediction engine.

Dataset

The AMR Atlas utilizes an curated aggregation of high-throughput genomic sequences derived from the PATRIC (Pathosystems Resource Integration Center) and NCBI databases. Our core training set comprises over 45,000 isolates across 12 bacterial species, including Staphylococcus aureus, Klebsiella pneumoniae, and Escherichia coli.

Data Composition

  • 45,210 Whole Genome Sequences (WGS)
  • Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC) phenotypic labels
  • Diversity spanning 85 countries over 15 years
  • Metadata including temporal and geographical tags

Feature Engineering

To transform raw genomic data into predictive features, we employ a multi-modal feature extraction strategy focusing on k-mer signatures and point mutations within known resistance-conferring genes.

terminal K-mer Profiling

Decomposition of sequences into overlapping substrings of length k=31. These are filtered by frequency to eliminate sequencing noise and rare variants.

fingerprint SNP Variants

Alignment-based identification of single nucleotide polymorphisms in target genes such as pbp2a and gyrA using the BWA-MEM algorithm.

Ranking Algorithm

To prioritize treatment recommendations, we apply a multi-objective ranking algorithm that balances predictive confidence with clinical guidelines and drug toxicity profiles.

Clinical Utility Score (CUS)
CUS(i) = (α * P_eff(i)) + (β * (1 - Tox(i))) + (γ * Guideline_Match(i))
Where:
• P_eff = Predicted probability of drug effectiveness
• Tox = Normalized toxicity index for the specific antibiotic
• α, β, γ = Weighting coefficients (Standard: 0.6, 0.2, 0.2)

Limitations

Data Bias

Our model is heavily reliant on existing database diversity. Rare resistance markers or emerging strains in under-sampled regions may show lower predictive accuracy.

Phenotypic Lag

The genotypic prediction does not always account for epigenetic factors or protein-level expressions that might alter the final phenotypic resistance.
